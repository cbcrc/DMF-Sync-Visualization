Slot = grain index mod 8. The slot is fixed by time, so a writer can't just append to the next free slot. now marks the slot for the current TAI grain and the amber head is the newest grain written. The gap between them is that flow's latency. A slot is overwritten when its grain is 8 grains old.

Four clocks, one time domain

Actual time (now)

The host's TAI clock, as nanoseconds since the SMPTE ST 2059-1 epoch. MXL uses that epoch and clock definition but doesn't require PTP. Any stable source works on one host. Across hosts, the clocks must not drift in frequency. Jitter is fine.

Origination time · OTS

The moment the media came into being: the shutter or the microphone capsule. Few transports carry it today. An RTP timestamp is at best a proxy. Cams 1 and 2 have a trustworthy one. Cam 3, on SDI, has none.

Indexing time stamp · ITS

The timestamp a grain carries inside the workload, phase-aligned to the grain rate. It is the index for alignment and selection, and it's carried unchanged through every media function. index = ⌊ts / 20'000'000⌋ slot = index mod 8

Presentation time · PT

When a synchronous egress (SDI, 2110 sender) emits the grain: PT = ITS + offset. The offset is fixed, so lip-sync and inter-camera timing hold at the output. It has to cover the slowest path, or the grain misses its slot.

These follow the draft JT-DMF ingress cases. Both clock and timestamps trusted: map them through. Clock trusted, timestamps not: stamp on arrival with a static offset. Neither trusted: a virtual frame store clocked by the DMF. The ingress work is still in progress.

Things to try
  1. Set Cam 3 static offset to 0. Cam 3's green origination ticks move one column left of its cells, with an arrow into the next column. Its frames are filed one index late, and the mix combines frames captured one grain apart.
  2. Drag Presentation offset to 2 grains (40 ms). The path from capture to ME output takes 47 ms, so every grain misses its presentation time and the output shows MISS.
  3. Turn RF jitter on and off. Cam 2's cells fill a little earlier or later but stay in the same column, because the index comes from origination time and not from arrival.
  4. Change NOW aligned to. At Origination the line is where cameras are capturing, and cells fill in behind it as grains reach MXL. At Presentation it sits on the column going to air, with the processed grains already waiting to its right. The slider sets anything in between.
  5. Pause and step ±10 ms, the same half-grain steps as the original slide.
